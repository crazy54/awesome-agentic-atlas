        The three ideas that explain everything

        What’s a quantisation (Q4, Q8)?

        A model is billions of numbers. Quantising stores each one with fewer digits, the way a JPEG shrinks a photo. Q4 keeps about 4 bits a number: roughly a quarter of the original size, and still very good. Q8 is closer to the original and twice as big. Below about 3 bits, answers get noticeably worse. We pick Q4 unless your machine needs smaller, and we say when it does.

        Why does context length cost memory?

        Context is how much the model can keep in mind at once: your conversation so far, or a document you paste in. It sets aside memory for that up front, a little for every word it might hold. We assume about 8,000 tokens (around 12 pages), which is plenty for a chat. Pasting in a whole book needs much more, and on a small machine that can be the difference between fitting and not.

        What does “offload” mean?

        A graphics card has its own fast memory. If a model doesn’t fit in it, part can live in your ordinary RAM instead and run on the main processor. It still works, but every word waits for the slow part, so it’s much slower than a model that fits on the card.

        Why does my Mac or handheld say less than its memory?

        Macs, Steam Decks and similar machines share one pool of memory between the processor and the graphics. That’s great for AI, because the graphics can use most of it, but the system keeps some for itself. On a Mac, macOS lets the graphics use about two-thirds of the memory by default (three quarters on bigger Macs).

        What’s a token?

        The piece of text a model reads and writes at a time: usually part of a word. One English word is about 1.3 tokens, so 10 tokens a second is about 7 or 8 words a second, faster than most people read.

        How we worked these numbers out

        A model needs its download size, plus memory for its context, plus about 0.6 GB to work in. Your machine offers a share of its memory, by a rule for each kind of machine. Speed comes from how fast your memory can be read, because every word means reading the whole active model once. We assume about 60% of the datasheet speed on a graphics card, and show a range of −30% to +30%. Real speed depends on the app, drivers and settings, so treat it as a guide. The code is open.
