How this page picks, and what it ignores

Every card here is something the index's first screen structurally never reaches: the median position of this week's 350 cards is #2996 of 8,858. That is not achieved by a popularity cut-off — the scheduler never reads stars. It deals from all 8,858 projects, oldest-featured first, so every one gets a turn every 182 days and the tail arrives here as often as the head.

The slots each category gets are weighted by the square root of its size, which is the one choice on this page worth arguing about. Proportional shares would hand the largest category half the page and rebuild the front page under a new heading; equal shares would exhaust the smallest category in a week while the largest took years to come round. Square root splits it, so all 14 categories appear every single day.

350 distinct projects fill this week's 350 slots. 131 have a screenshot, so 219 card faces are drawn from the project's own facts instead.

A committed ledger records when each project was last featured, which is what makes the rotation a rotation rather than a shuffle with no memory. All seven days are baked into this page and the client picks between them, because the deploy is weekly and the rollover is at your midnight — a page containing only today would still be showing Sunday on Thursday. Seven is the hard ceiling on what is reachable, not this week's coincidence: there is no archive and no date to type, so the days you can open are the days in the file you were served.
